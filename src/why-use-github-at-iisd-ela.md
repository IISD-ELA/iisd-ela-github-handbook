# 1. Why use GitHub at IISD-ELA?
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;IISD-ELA has developed a wealth of knowledge across many research areas over decades, and much of this work is valuable beyond the individual projects or teams that originally developed it. **GitHub provides a centralized organized space where we can preserve, share, discover, and build upon this work within and beyond the organization**. It helps us reduce duplicated effort, establish consistent practices, support collaboration, and ensure valuable resources are accessible for both internal and external researchers, projects, and other staff.

Expand each point to learn more. 
<details>
<summary><strong>Establish Consistent Scientific Methods and Practices</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Different teams or researchers may use different approaches to accomplish the same task. By maintaining agreed-upon methods and resources in a central location, we can encourage greater consistency across IISD-ELA.  

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: There are several ways to calculate lake thermocline depth. If there is a consensus method available on our GitHub, staff can use the same code (e.g., R script) rather than developing their own. This saves us time and ensures that results are consistent and comparable across projects.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Support Reproducible and Transparent Research</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Making the methods, tools, and workflows behind our research accessible helps others understand how results were produced, verify findings, and reproduce analyses.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: A publishing researcher can share the analytical methods and scripts used to generate the results on their publication. A DOI can be obtained for a version of their GitHub repository containing these methods, and the work can be formally cited in the publication. This supports transparency and makes it easier for other researchers to build on existing findings.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Provide a Simple Way to Preserve and Share Work</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Not every project needs to be actively maintained, highly technical, or intended for publications to be worth preserving. GitHub can serve as a home for completed projects, useful tools, documented methods, reports, and other resources that may benefit colleagues.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: You have a collection of scripts or a workflow developed for a project. Even if you may not plan to use it again now, making it available on GitHub could save someone else or future you considerable time. This will also prevent local files from getting lost if your computer breaks down or you switch to a new work laptop.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Reduce Duplicated Effort and Encourage Reuse</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Different teams may encounter similar challenges without knowing that another team has already developed a solution. By making existing work easier to find, GitHub helps us identify resources we can reuse or adapt, saving time and reducing duplication.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: A team needs to establish a quality-control procedure for their data. Rather than developing an entirely new approach, they may discover an existing workflow from another staff member or team that can be reused or adapted.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Improve Collaboration and Maintain a History of Work</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;GitHub provides a shared environment where staff can contribute to projects, suggest improvements, and track how files change over time. Instead of maintaining multiple copies of files across personal folders, teams or individuals can work from a common version with a documented history of changes.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: Several staff contribute to a shared analytical procedure. GitHub allows them to propose updates, review changes, and retain earlier versions. This makes it easier to understand how and why the procedure has evolved.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Make Organizational Resources Discoverable</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Useful resources developed by one team may not be widely known to others. Our GitHub organization brings these resources together and uses standardized topic tags to make them discoverable. When appropriate, repositories can also be made public to support collaboration and knowledge sharing with external researchers.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example**: A researcher working on a new hydrology project can explore existing IISD-ELA repositories tagged with the "hydrolim" topic tag to find relevant workflows developed by colleagues.*
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Preserve Institutional Knowledge</strong></summary>
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Valuable knowledge and resources may become difficult to locate or access as projects conclude, staff retire or change roles, or teams evolve. GitHub provides an organization-managed location where important methods, tools, documentation, and project resources can remain available beyond the involvement of any one individual.

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;***Example:** A staff member develops a procedure for processing data collected by a particular field instrument but retires or leaves the organization. If that procedure is uploaded and documented on GitHub, future staff can understand and reuse it without having to reconstruct the process from scratch.* 
</details>

---
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;The bottom line is that GitHub is a way for us to make better use of the knowledge, methods, and resources we already produce. It helps us preserve our work, improve consistency, encourage collaboration, and make it easier for staff and future researchers to benefit from what others have already accomplished.
